# Quieter

Quiet design is harder than bold design. Subtlety needs precision. Reduce visual intensity in designs that are too loud, aggressive, or overstimulating without losing personality or making the result generic.

## Visitor mode

- **Persuade + Experience:** "quieter" means a more restrained palette, more whitespace, more typographic air. Drama is reduced, not eliminated; the POV stays intact.
- **Operate + Read:** "quieter" means reducing visual noise — fewer background accents, flatter cards, less color, less motion. The tool should disappear more completely into the task.

## Assess the current state

Analyze what makes the design feel too intense:

- **Intensity sources:** color saturation; contrast extremes; visual weight (too many bold, heavy elements competing); animation excess; complexity (too many elements, patterns, decorations); scale (everything large and loud, no hierarchy).
- **Context:** What's the purpose (marketing vs tool vs reading)? Who's the audience — some contexts need energy. What's working — don't throw away good ideas. What's the core message — preserve what matters.

If any of these are unclear from the codebase, do not guess — ask.

"Quieter" means refined and easier on the eyes, not generic. Generic quiet looks like gray text on white, one sans at a uniform weight, and a grid of identical cards; restraint still needs a point of view.

## Plan the refinement

Decide before editing: the color approach (desaturate, or shift to restrained tones?), the hierarchy approach (which very few elements stay bold, which recede?), the simplification approach (what can be removed entirely?), and the sophistication approach (how to signal quality through restraint). Subtlety requires precision — quiet without intent collapses to generic.

## Refine

**Color** — Reduce saturation (shift from fully saturated to 70-85%). Replace bright colors with muted tones. Use fewer colors more thoughtfully; let neutrals do more work and color act as accent. Keep high contrast only where it matters most. Use warm or cool tinted grays instead of pure gray — depth without loudness. Never gray text on a colored background: use a darker shade of that color, or transparency, instead.

**Visual weight** — Reduce font weights (900 → 600, 700 → 500) and sizes where appropriate. Build hierarchy through weight, size, and space instead of color and boldness. Increase breathing room; reduce density. Reduce border thickness and opacity, or remove borders entirely.

**Simplification** — Remove decorative elements: gradients, shadows, patterns, textures that don't serve a purpose. Simplify shapes and border-radius extremes. Flatten layering. Reduce or remove blur effects, glows, and stacked shadows.

**Motion** — Reduce animation intensity: shorter distances (10-20px instead of 40px), gentler easing. Remove decorative animations; keep functional motion. Replace dramatic effects with gentle feedback; use ease-out curves for understated motion — never bounce or elastic. Remove animations entirely if they serve no clear purpose.

**Composition** — Reduce scale jumps: smaller contrast between sizes creates a calmer feeling. Bring rogue elements back into systematic alignment. Replace extreme spacing variations with a consistent rhythm.

**Avoid:** making everything the same size/weight (hierarchy still matters); removing all color (quiet ≠ grayscale); eliminating all personality (maintain character through refinement); sacrificing usability for aesthetics (functional elements still need clear affordances); making everything small and light (some anchors are needed).

## Verify

- **Still functional:** can users still accomplish tasks easily?
- **Still distinctive:** does it have character, or is it generic now?
- **Better reading:** is text easier to read for extended periods?
- **Restrained, not absent:** does the POV survive the cuts?

When the result feels right, finish with `/impeccable polish`.
